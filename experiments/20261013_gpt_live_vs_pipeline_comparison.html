How much to trust this

  • The latency method is checked. For the pipeline the matching method gives s on average, and its own timestamps give s. The same method is applied to gpt-live-1, so the gap between the two is reliable even though each absolute number carries a little error.
  • The reference is not human-checked. English came from a careful Gemini transcription of the clip. The Japanese was written by Claude from that transcript. Neither system under test wrote it, but a bilingual reviewer should confirm it before these scores go in a paper.
  • Three clips, one lecture, one speaker style. Quality is a 3-of-3 win, but the gap on clip A (about 2 points) is smaller than the 3.6-point spread between two gpt-live-1 runs of the same clip.
  • gpt-live-1 had no dictionary or context. The pipeline did. A prompt with the glossary may close part of the quality gap, and a faster interpreter setting may shorten the delay. Neither is tested here.
  • The anomaly gate passed everywhere. No card exceeded 8 units/s and no window had four or more cards within 3 seconds, for either system.
